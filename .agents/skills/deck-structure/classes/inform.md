# Class guide: inform

The audience must understand something or learn a process. Address likely questions and misunderstandings rather than arguing for a disputed recommendation.

Sources: Lumen Learning's four informative types (definitional, descriptive, explanatory, demonstration), each with its own order.

## Choose a subtype

Use the primary outcome in `purpose.md`, the audience profile, and the brief to choose. Record consequential assumptions; ask only if a missing distinction blocks useful work.

- **Define:** explain what a term or concept means.
- **Describe:** give a vivid picture of a thing, person, or place.
- **Explain:** say how or why something happens, and what follows.
- **Demonstrate:** show how a process is done. Use this when the audience will do it.

## Sections, in order

Record unavailable facts as `unknown` and missing evidence as `gap`.

1. **Subtype.** One of the four above.
2. **Finding.** The main thing the audience will know or be able to do after the talk. One sentence.
3. **Main points.** Include what the audience needs, in the subtype's order:
   - *Define:* establish the meaning, then use classification, contrast, analogy, history, or relevance as useful.
   - *Describe:* describe the distinguishing features, using sensory detail or comparison where helpful.
   - *Explain:* establish the cause or mechanism before its consequences; add related context where needed. Mark an unknown cause `unknown` rather than inventing it.
   - *Demonstrate:* order the necessary process steps, each with a signpost and a claim about what it achieves. Note safety risks and steps that need a visual where applicable.
4. **Likely questions.** Map relevant questions from `audience.md` or the brief to main points; record an unavailable answer as `unknown`. Record `not used` if none are likely, or `unknown` if they are not known.

## Opening and close

Choose an opening suited to the audience: the Finding, a relevant question, or an example. Make the Finding clear before the details, follow the subtype's main-point order, and answer likely questions where they arise. Close by restating what the audience can now explain or do; a demonstration may name a next practice step.
