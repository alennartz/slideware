# Class guide: persuade

The audience must decide, approve, act, or change a belief.

Sources: Minto's Pyramid Principle (answer first), Toulmin (claim, grounds, rebuttal), Duarte (contrast between what is and what could be).

## Sections, in order

Use `purpose.md`, `audience.md`, and the brief to draft; ask only if missing information blocks useful work. Record unavailable facts as `unknown` and missing evidence as `gap`.

1. **Answer.** The claim the audience must accept to reach the primary outcome. One sentence.
2. **Tension.** Where a meaningful contrast exists, describe "what is" from the problem in `purpose.md` and "what could be" once the outcome holds. If no contrast applies, record `not used`.
3. **Supports.** The reasons needed to establish the answer, each a claim sentence. Record `unknown` if the reasons are unavailable rather than inventing them.
4. **Objection map.** Map each objection in `audience.md` to a support or rebuttal, carrying over its evidence or gap. If an answer is unavailable, record it as `unknown`. If there are no objections, record `not used`; if objections are unknown, record `unknown`.

## Opening and close

Choose answer-first or, when tension applies, tension-first to suit the audience and brief. Keep the supports and objection answers after the answer. Close with the decision, action, or belief requested by the primary outcome.
